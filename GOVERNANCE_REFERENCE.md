## 2. Vote thresholds (of 15)

- **Quorum:** 10 (two-thirds, per Amendment HH 2024).
- **Pass ordinary action / ordinance:** 8 (majority of full membership — majority of those *present* is never enough).
- **Override mayoral veto (ordinary ordinance):** 10 (two-thirds).
- **Override (supermajority-origin ordinance) / urgency ordinance:** 12 (three-fourths).
- **Override mayoral *budget* line-item veto:** 10 (two-thirds), within 5 working days.
- **Assert jurisdiction over a board/commission action:** 10.
- **Tap Emergency Reserve:** 10 ("urgent economic necessity").

## 3. Mayor's powers (Charter Art. II)

Chief executive over ~44 departments; proposes budget; **line-item budget veto** + ordinance veto; appoints GMs and commissioners (council-confirmed, 45-day default-approval window); may remove most appointees; issues **Executive Directives** (binding, effective 15 days after publication); declares emergencies; annual State of the City. Cannot legislate; vetoes overridable at 10; Controller & City Attorney are independent electeds; proprietary boards (DWP, Harbor, Airports) pick their own GMs.

## 4. Council powers & process

Legislate by **ordinance** (force of law, vetoable) and **resolution** (position, not vetoable). Path: motion/request → City Clerk assigns **Council File #** → standing committee hearing(s) + dept reports + public comment → committee report → full council vote → (ordinances) to Mayor → sign / veto / lapse-to-law after 10 days → override window. Ordinances generally held one week before passage unless unanimous.

**Standing committees (June 2026 chairs):** Budget & Finance (Yaroslavsky); PLUM / land-use (Blumenfield — *most powerful, most scandal-prone*); Public Safety (Lee); Housing & Homelessness (Raman); Transportation (Hutt); Energy & Environment (Nazarian); Public Works (Hernandez); Civil Rights/Immigration (Soto-Martínez); Economic Development & Jobs (Price→successor); Government Operations (Padilla); Personnel & Hiring (McOsker); Arts/Parks/Libraries (Jurado); Trade/Travel/Tourism (Park); Rules & Elections (Harris-Dawson). Ad hoc: 2028 Olympics; LA Recovery (Park); Unarmed Crisis Response.

## 5. Budget cycle (FY Jul 1–Jun 30)

~Feb 1 Mayor priorities → **by Apr 20 Mayor submits proposed budget** → Budget & Finance dept-by-dept hearings → **by Jun 1 Council adopts/modifies (majority=8)** → +5 working days Mayor signs or line-item vetoes → +5 days Council overrides (10) → Jul 1 FY begins. If Council misses the deadline, the Mayor's version becomes law. No voter approval. **CAO** (serves Mayor+Council, prepares/administers budget, negotiates labor) and **CLA** (Council's own analyst) jointly staff Budget & Finance. **Emergency Reserve** floor = 2.75% of GF; policy target total reserve ≥5%.

## 6. Individual member levers

District discretionary funds (Community Services ~$94k/district; AB1290 redevelopment; street-furniture; real-property trust). **Councilmanic prerogative** — unwritten norm where the full council defers to the district member on local land-use/zoning; reciprocal deference makes a single member able to make/break projects. This is the corruption engine (Huizar/PLUM pay-to-play, 13 yrs; Englander 14 mo; Ridley-Thomas 42 mo). Constituent services run from district offices.

## 7. Neighborhood Councils

99 advisory NCs (Charter Art. IX), elected volunteer boards, ~$32k/yr each, supported by EmpowerLA/DONE, overseen by BONC. Power = **Community Impact Statements** filed into the Council File system + early notice + testimony. On land use the member's prerogative outweighs the NC.

## 8. Ethics & scandal model

City Ethics Commission (5 members). Gift cap $630/yr (state) — stricter LA caps: $100/yr restricted sources, $25/yr lobbyist. Form 700 annual disclosure. Lobbying registration at 30+ hrs/3 months. 2024 voter-approved reforms: **DD** (independent redistricting commission), **ER** (ethics budget $6.5M, max penalty $15k, 180-day response rule), **HH** (conflict disclosure pre-confirmation, §244 quorum change). **2026 Charter Reform Commission** (final report Apr 2, 2026) proposes **expanding council to ~25 seats**, stronger mayor, LAPD-oversight overhaul, land-use/ethics rewrite — council decides whether to put on Nov 2026 ballot.

## Design intent (how this maps to mechanics)

- **Coalition engine:** each council member has an ideology vector + a 0–100 support-for-you that shifts with your votes, favors, whipping (political capital), and committee posture. Initiatives pass at 8, etc.
- **Biweekly turn loop** from Dec 14 2026; player actions cost Action Points and either trigger or shift event probabilities. Charter dates (budget Apr 20 / Jun 1) are fixed calendar beats.
- **Three win vectors:** agenda (ordinances passed & signed), approval/re-election (2030), crisis management — tracked as a legacy score.
- **Councilmanic-prerogative / PLUM** = the capital-vs-scandal temptation (accept developer help for fast capital + rising scandal risk → possible federal-probe crisis).
